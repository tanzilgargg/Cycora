Cycora

A two-phase 2D game where players run a busy kitchen first, then sort leftover food after the shift.

Overview

I made Cycora as a typing game about one full restaurant shift. In the first phase, players take orders, move through kitchen stations, and serve food before customers get impatient. In the second phase, they sort leftover food and packaging into donation, compost, recycle, or trash. I made it for younger players, so I kept the prompts clear, the typing simple, and the learning goal easy to understand.

Two-Phase Overview

Phase 1 is about taking orders, preparing dishes, and serving meals fast. Phase 2 is about sorting leftovers into the best recovery channel before time runs out.

Why This Project Matters

The game shows that leftovers are not all the same. Some food can still help people, some materials can be reused, and trash should be the last option.

Accessibility Features

Players can turn on larger text and high-contrast prompts, and new prompts briefly pause the timer so there is more time to read.

System 1

Service Phase

The service phase lasts about 120 seconds. Customers arrive, wait at tables, and lose patience if the player takes too long. Players type the prompt for a guest, send the dish into the kitchen, complete each station step in order, and then serve the meal.

Timed Rush

A 120-second timer sets the pace. The HUD shows time, profit, impact, meals served, and combo while the kitchen gets busier.

Guest Flow and Patience

Customers sit at tables, wait for service, and leave if their patience runs out. This helps players decide who they should help first.

Order Taking

Typing the order word confirms the dish, adds it to the queue, and gives a small patience reset before prep starts.

Cooking Pipeline

Recipes follow fixed station paths like soup: fridge, chop, sink, stove, plate, serve. Players follow the highlighted station instead of choosing the order themselves.

Serving

After prep is done, players type the final serve action and match the meal to the correct guest.

Combo and Reward System

Correct orders, prep steps, serves, and later correct sorting choices build combo. Losing a guest or making mistakes breaks the streak.

Controls and Flow

The first dishes are introduced in a guided order, starting with soup and then sandwich. After that, dishes come from a larger pool, so players have to watch the active prompt and react to what the kitchen needs next.

System 2

Recovery Phase

After the rush ends, the game moves into leftover sorting. Each item appears as a card with an icon and a hint, and players type one of four destination words: donation, compost, recycle, or trash. It is slower than service, but the timer still matters.

Transition from Service to Recovery

When the service timer ends, the kitchen phase stops, the remaining guests leave, and the game moves into cleanup.

Timed Leftover Sorting

A second timer runs during sorting. If time ends before all leftovers are sorted, the unsorted ones count as missed sorts.

Four Recovery Channels

Donation is for safe food, compost is for organic scraps, recycle is for clean packaging, and trash is the last option.

Reading Context and Making Decisions

Players have to read the card hint before typing. The goal is not just to guess fast, but to match the item to the correct destination.

Scoring and Impact

Correct sorts increase impact, donation-worthy food adds more value, and wrong sorts or missed leftovers lower the final result.

Donation Mechanic

Donation is special because it only counts when food is still safe for people. The game tracks donation items to show that some leftovers can still help others.

Visual Design

Readable, Fast, Distinct

This section focuses on the interface, readability, and the final visual style.

Page Intro

The interface is designed to help players read quickly. It uses station labels, table areas, prompts, and a clear HUD so players can understand what to type next.

Visual Language

Highlighted stations, simple icons, and clear prompt words help players follow the recipe order without guessing where to go next.

Interface Design

The HUD shows time, profit, impact, meals served, and combo during service, then shifts to recovery choices and hints in the sorting phase.

Phase Contrast

The service phase looks busier because tables, recipes, and timing all compete for attention. The recovery phase is calmer and focuses on one item card at a time.

Accessibility

Large prompts, high-contrast prompts, and a short reading pause when a new word appears help players read before reacting.

Process and Iteration

Wireframes and layout tests helped me figure out where tables, stations, dialogue, typing input, and recovery cards should go so the screen stays readable.

Concept

Labor and Waste in One Loop

This section explains the main idea behind the project and what I wanted players to learn.

Core Idea

I made Cycora as an educational typing game about food waste, leftovers, and food access. I wanted players to learn by doing instead of only reading about it.

Why Restaurants?

I chose a restaurant shift because it is easy for younger players to understand. It has clear roles, visible pressure, and obvious leftovers. It also lets me put service work and waste decisions in the same setting.

Why Typing?

I chose typing because it connects reading and action in a simple way. Players see a word and use it right away to take an order, complete a recipe step, or sort a leftover.

Circular Economy Framing

I used four destinations in the recovery phase because leftovers should not all be treated the same. Donation, compost, recycle, and trash each show a different next step for food or packaging.

Learning and Takeaway

My main takeaway is that speed matters in a kitchen, but so do the choices made after service. I wanted the project to show that leftovers can still be useful, and that a better result comes from serving well and sorting carefully.
